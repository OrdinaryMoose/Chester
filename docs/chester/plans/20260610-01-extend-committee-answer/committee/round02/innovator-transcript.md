## Engaging the round01 synthesis

The split is placement-only. Three positions (including mine) want typed warrant; one (Conservator) wants warrant-type labeling folded into `rationale` prose. The substance is converged. So the only live question is: does a discrete field earn its schema-surface cost?

### Engaging Conservator's surface-widening concern directly

Conservator argues: adding a fourth field to a C-RIGID-boundary schema widens the Consolidator's surface. True. But the widening is bounded and asymmetric:

- The Consolidator is enumerate-only. Adding one more named field means it enumerates one more chunk — the warrant text. It does not interpret; it cannot misread a field it merely copies. The surface widens by one field, not by one reasoning task.
- Folding warrant into `rationale` creates an *unbounded* reading surface. The Consolidator must now parse a denser prose block and determine where rationale ends and warrant begins — a distinction that does not exist structurally if they share a field. That parsing is interpretation, not enumeration. Conservator's option avoids adding a field but adds a parsing obligation that the enumerate-only rule does not cover cleanly.
- The C-RIGID constraint protects routing signals and Consolidator boundary behavior. A fourth content field does not touch routing and adds a fixed-width enumerable chunk to what Consolidator copies. That is within the "content extension" carve-out the dispatch already grants.

The surface-widening concern is real but points the wrong direction: the narrower surface is the discrete field, not the extended-rationale field. Extended prose adds interpretive surface; a typed discrete field adds enumerable surface.

### Re-framing: the warrant field IS the answer-contribution unit

The synthesis asks whether a re-framing dissolves the placement question. There is one.

The original dispatch says: members shift advocacy → answer-contribution; each proposes a warranted piece supporting the lead's answer goal. Under the current schema, `position + rationale + blocking_risk` encode the advocacy artifact. The warrant is the answer-contribution artifact. They are not the same thing.

If the warrant field IS the answer-contribution unit — the discrete output the team-lead picks up to verify against the answer body — then the placement question is already answered: it cannot live inside `rationale` because that field encodes the advocacy-stance reasoning, not the answer-contribution output. Folding warrant into rationale makes the answer-contribution unit invisible: the team-lead must excavate it from advocacy prose rather than read it as a first-class output.

This reframe is not novelty. It follows from the dispatch's own framing: "each proposes a warranted piece supporting the lead's answer goal." A piece is a discrete output. Embedding a piece inside advocacy prose collapses the distinction the dispatch just created.

This is the warrant: the dispatch structurally designates the warrant as the answer-contribution unit; a discrete field surfaces that unit; folding it into rationale buries it.
