The finding, made precise

This is not "two files happen to overlap." One file cites the other as the authority and then overrides it in the same section.

SKILL.md claims canonical

"The canonical per-round sequence (spec §5). Steps 1–3 are member-side; steps 4–8 are team-lead-side (detail in references/team-lead.md)."

SKILL.md:179–180

SKILL.md owns the 8-step skeleton and explicitly delegates the team-lead-side detail downward.

team-lead.md defers — then overrides

"Per-round flow runs — per SKILL.md Phase 4 § Per-Round Flow. Members write their full positions…"

team-lead.md:96 — its own step 2

team-lead.md's own step 2 names SKILL.md Phase 4 as the authority — yet team-lead.md:93–106 re-numbers the entire round 1 through 11, re-skeletoning the very thing step 2 just delegated upward.

So the fix direction is not a coin-flip. Both files already agree, in writing, that SKILL.md Phase 4 is the owner. The drift is purely that team-lead.md re-expanded a rival numbered list instead of elaborating SKILL's steps by name. The fix is to make the prose match the deference both files already declare.

Bonus finding — dangling citation. SKILL.md:179 attributes the sequence to "(spec §5)", but no spec document exists anywhere under skills/design-committee/ (grep-confirmed). It is a vestigial pointer to an original design spec that was never carried into the tree. Either drop the parenthetical or repoint it at the real owner once that's decided.

The design fork — who owns the numbered list

Direction A — team-lead.md owns the full numbered list; SKILL.md cites it

Not recommended

Gives the executing agent one complete list in the file it already reads for role behaviour. But:

  • Inverts a deference already in writing. team-lead.md:96 names SKILL.md Phase 4 the authority — A would flip that.
  • Demotes the floor. SKILL.md is the contract surface wrapping skills compare against (skill-contract.md). Moving the canonical sequence into a reference thins the floor.
  • Members never read team-lead.md. Steps 1–3 are member-side; members read member-protocol, not team-lead. The member-visible skeleton belongs in the floor doc, not a team-lead-only file.

Direction B — SKILL.md Phase 4 owns the 8 steps; team-lead.md elaborates by name

Recommended

SKILL.md keeps the single numbered skeleton. team-lead.md drops its 1–11 list and instead attaches its operational detail to SKILL's step names, with ledger and designer-response living in the surrounding Conversation Loop / § Ledger where they already sit.

  • Honors existing intent. Matches both team-lead.md:96 (defers up) and SKILL.md:180 (delegates detail down).
  • Floor stays canonical. One numbered sequence, in the contract doc.
  • No rival numbers to drift. team-lead.md has no step integers left to fall out of sync.

The deepened interface

What "read this" buys a reader after the fix, per file.

SKILL.md § Phase 4 — Per-Round Flow

Owns: the 8 numbered steps (1 Dispatch → 8 Present) as the sole canonical sequence; the member-side / team-lead-side split note; the checkpoint invariant. Keeps delegating: "team-lead-side detail in team-lead.md" (already there, line 180). Cleanup: drop or repoint the dangling "(spec §5)".

team-lead.md § Per-Round Flow

Becomes: per-step operational elaboration keyed to SKILL's step names — which Consolidator to dispatch (under Consolidate), evict-from-context discipline (under Synthesize/Converge), warrant record (under Converge), off-roster scribe dispatch (under Author). Loses: the 1–11 integers. No information lost — the detail stays, it just stops carrying rival step numbers.

team-lead.md § Ledger & § Conversation Loop

Absorbs: the ledger-update (old step 3) as the round-boundary bookkeeping it already describes, and the designer-response branch (old step 11) as the outer loop cycle it already frames. Neither was ever an inner-round deliberation step.

Migration risk & the deletion test

Risk = zero external breakage

  • Grep across skills/ agents/ docs/: no file cites "step N" of either committee per-round flow. The only references are the two internal ones being reconciled.
  • No wrapping skill exists yet (design-architect-committee is a future example in skill-contract.md, not a live file). Nothing downstream pins the numbering.
  • This is documentation-internal coherence — no runtime code path changes.

Deletion test on team-lead's 1–11 list

Delete team-lead.md's numbered list. Does complexity vanish or relocate? It relocates cleanly: the deliberation steps were already owned by SKILL.md (team-lead's own step 2 says so); the detail re-attaches by name; bookkeeping and the outer loop already have homes. Complexity does not reappear across callers — there are none. That "deletes without reappearing" is the signal the rival list was redundant scaffolding, not load-bearing.

Open questions for you

Three decisions before this becomes a plan

  1. Direction. Confirm Direction B (SKILL.md owns the 8 steps; team-lead.md elaborates by name) over Direction A (team-lead owns the full list). The existing-intent evidence points to B — is there a reason you'd want the executor's single-file convenience of A instead?
  2. The ledger & designer-response steps. Confirm they leave the inner numbered sequence entirely and live only in § Ledger / § Conversation Loop — or do you want them kept visible as explicit "between-round" steps adjacent to the 8, just un-numbered?
  3. The dangling "(spec §5)". Drop the parenthetical, or is there an original design spec it should be repointed at (and carried into the tree)?

Answer these and the change is a tight, single-purpose edit to two files with no code and no external migration.
